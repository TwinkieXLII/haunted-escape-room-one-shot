# Station 3: The Clockwork Observatory — Of Gears and Gadgets

## Source & Metadata
*   **Source:** *The Game Master's Book of Traps, Puzzles and Dungeons* (Jeff Ashworth), Ch 12 Gateway Puzzles Part 2, pp. 142+ (exact page not recorded). Book mechanic: place gears with 4, 7, 7, 3 teeth on pegs 1–4, solved from a plaque riddle beginning *"Four in one and three in four..."*; the book lists no failure cost. Replaces the pure dice-rolling ghost puzzle. The plaque riddle below is an original rewrite for this table; the book's line is held back as the Clue 4 bailout.
*   **Optional Add-On:** *Noon Mark* (Ch 12) if the pair finishes early (see DM Tips).
*   **Estimated Playtime:** 10–12 minutes (Simulated Playtest: **11m 20s** — within the 10–15 min target; see [Station 3 Playtest Report](Playtests/Station_3_Playtest.md)).
*   **Type:** Riddle decoding, star-chart reading, tooth counting, and a simple sum check, under light combat pressure.
*   **Players:** Count Dracula / Drac (Bard 5) & Johnny (Bard 5).
*   **Goal:** Place the correct gears on the four door pegs in the right order and turn the crank to unlock the vault, lock the Clockwork Phantom back into its clock, and release **Crest #3**.

## DM Notes (Coaching) — DM ONLY

> **Table visibility key:** Sections marked **DM ONLY** never go on the table. Only the items marked **PLAYER HANDOUT** in the props section (plaque card, star chart, gear tokens, door board, phantom standee, crest token) are shown to players. Keep this page, the Solution Matrix, and the pencil marks on the gear backs out of sight.

### What this room is testing
This room tests whether the pair can **translate between three things that look unrelated**: a riddle (words), a star chart (pictures), and a pile of gears (objects). The riddle names three constellations without saying their names; the chart shows how many stars each one has; and the hidden rule is that **each star becomes one gear tooth**. The phantom is there to add urgency and to give the Bards' powers something to do. It is not the challenge. A good run has one "wait, the *wyrm* is the Dragon!" moment, one "stars are teeth!" moment, and a satisfying crank at the end.

### How to describe the room (read-aloud)
> *"The stairs end in a round brass observatory. Above you, the domed ceiling is painted with a night sky, and six constellations glitter in gold and grey. A huge brass vault door fills the far wall. It has four empty pegs in a row and a crank handle in the middle. On the worktable, loose brass gears are scattered beside a dusty astrolabe. Then the big clock on the wall strikes, and something steps out of it: an empty suit of armor, held together by spinning gears, swinging a pendulum like a scythe. Tick. Tick. Tick."*

Then put the **door board**, the **seven gear tokens** (in a jumbled pile) and the **phantom standee** on the table. Hand over the **plaque card** when anyone looks at the door. Hand over the **star chart** when anyone touches the astrolabe or the desk, or at Clue 1.

### Running the phantom as pressure, not the main event
*   Use a phone timer. The phantom **strikes on a real-time tick: first at 3:00, then every 2 minutes** (5:00, 7:00, 9:00, 11:00...), plus **once immediately whenever they turn the crank with a wrong set**.
*   Narrate each tick in one line ("TICK... the scythe swings at Johnny!"), roll, resolve, and go straight back to the puzzle. Do not run initiative.
*   Alternate targets. If a character drops below 10 HP, have the phantom "steal time" instead (it moves one gear back to the pile) so nobody goes down.
*   If they want to fight it, let them (see derails), but it simply reassembles.

### Non-spoiler nudge lines (say these aloud; they redirect attention, never give the answer)
| Stage | When to use it | Say this |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Orientation | 0:00–3:00, they're only looking at gears | "What else in this room has the same gold color as the door?" · "Who's checked the astrolabe on the desk?" |
| Decoding names | 3:00–6:00, stuck on riddle words | "Which picture on the chart could be called that?" · "Is there more than one thing on the chart that has wings? Where's the arrow?" · "What does a reaper carry?" |
| Character features | 5:00–6:00, Drac hasn't used his sheet | "Drac, look at your Vampire Powers. Anything that gets you up to that ceiling?" |
| Stars to gears | 6:00–8:00, they know the constellations but not what to *do* with them | "The riddle says the stars *bite*. What on this table bites?" · "Count something on the chart, then count something on the table." |
| Checking work | Any time they have a full set | "Before you crank it, does the riddle give you any way to check your answer?" · "What's in the middle of the chart?" |

### What NOT to say or do
*   Never say a number of teeth or stars aloud before Clue 4. Don't count the Dragon's stars for them, even if they miscount; ask them to recount.
*   Never say "stars equal teeth" before Clue 3. Let the riddle's "bite" line or Clue 3 do that.
*   Don't confirm or deny individual gears ("that one's right"). Only a full crank gives feedback.
*   Don't flip the gear tokens over; the pencil marks on the backs are for you.
*   Don't let the phantom take more than about 30 seconds of any 2 minutes.
*   Don't skip ahead to Clue 4 just because the room is quiet. Quiet usually means they're counting.

### Pacing checkpoints
| Clock | Where they should be | What you do if they're behind |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **2:00–3:00** | They have both handouts and are reading the riddle against the chart. | Deliver **Clue 1** (the dome flickers). |
| **3:00** | First phantom tick. | Roll the first strike. |
| **5:00–6:00** | They've matched at least the Dragon to "the great wyrm". | Prompt Drac's sheet, then **Clue 2** (Bat Form). |
| **7:00** | They're counting stars. | Use the "what bites?" nudge. |
| **8:00–9:00** | They have a full set of four gears in mind. | **Clue 3** (crank inscription). |
| **11:00** | The door should be open or about to open. | **Clue 4** (fail-forward). |
| **13:00** | — | Hard bailout: the hypnotized phantom points to each gear in turn. Let them place them and crank, then move on. |

### Handling derails
*   **Attacking the phantom instead of solving:** Allow it; it's fun. The phantom has **AC 15, 22 HP**. If it drops to 0, it bursts into gears and **skips its next tick**, then reassembles. Knocking it down buys time but never opens the door. After one round of attacks, say: "The pieces are already crawling back together. The *door* is what locks it away."
*   **Brute-forcing the crank:** Each wrong crank costs a strike. After the second wrong crank, use the "does the riddle give you a way to check?" nudge.
*   **Clever alternative solutions (the book says reward them):** Say yes to good reasoning. Examples:
    *   Drac questions or intimidates the phantom (Persuasion/Intimidation +7 vs DC 15): it ticks once for every tooth on **one** peg of your choice. Give them Peg 1.
    *   They reason out the twins and the 21 total before counting every constellation: great, let it stand.
    *   Prying the door or smashing the pegs: the door is magical and won't move (no roll needed). Say so kindly and redirect.
*   **Arguing about riddle wording** (Raven vs. Bat, Coffin vs. Scythe): Let it run up to about 90 seconds; it's good table energy. Then ask "Where does the arrow point?" or bring in Clue 2.

### Adjudicating the character hooks (checked against the character sheets)
Each PC has **3 USES** boxes shared by their abilities. If pairs rotate through several stations, treat each new station as a short rest and refresh the boxes.
*   **Drac — Look Into My Eyes!** (Action, 1 use): the phantom makes a DC 15 Wisdom save (**its bonus is +0**). On a failure it is Paralyzed for 1 turn, so it **skips its next strike** (tick or crank). From 11:00 on, a successful freeze also triggers **Clue 4**.
*   **Drac — Bat Form** (Bonus action, 1 use): flies up to the dome and delivers **Clue 2**.
*   **Drac — Inspiring Bleh** (Bonus, 1 use): a d8 for Johnny's next roll or save. Fine to use on any check here.
*   **Johnny — DJ Backpack** (Reaction, 1 use): when the phantom attacks Johnny, the attack still resolves, but the phantom is stunned and **skips its next strike**, including a crank strike.
*   **Johnny — Sick Beats** (his spell attack; DC 15 Wisdom save, 2d6 psychic). **Once at this station**, after a wrong crank, Johnny can use his action on Sick Beats to break the phantom's rhythm: **that crank's strike is cancelled** and the gears pop back out with no penalty, whether or not the save succeeds.
*   **Johnny — Chill Vibes / Monster Mash:** a d8 for any roll, or 1d8 healing for friends within 6 squares. Use them freely if the phantom has landed hits.
*   **Johnny's History (+3), DC 12:** backup route to **Clue 2** if Drac won't use Bat Form.

## Room Setup — DM ONLY
*   A massive brass vault door has **four drive pegs**, labeled **Peg 1, Peg 2, Peg 3, and Peg 4**, and a **crank** in the center. A small engraved arrow (↑) sits above Peg 1.
*   A bronze plaque above the pegs bears the riddle (**Prop Handout 1**).
*   The domed ceiling shows the same six constellations as the **Astrolabe Star Chart (Prop Handout 2)**, found on the worktable next to the gears. Only three of the painted constellations are gilded in "celestial brass" (seen only up close; see Clue 2).
*   **Seven brass gears** lie on the worktable. They are **all the same polished brass, with no stamps or markings on the front**, so tooth count is the only difference:
    *   **Required Gears:** one 4-tooth, two 7-tooth, one 3-tooth.
    *   **Decoy Gears:** one 5-tooth, one 6-tooth, one 8-tooth (they match the three decoy constellations on the chart).
*   **The Hazard:** an 8-foot-tall Clockwork Phantom (floating armor with whirring clockwork and a pendulum scythe) hovers in the room, ticking louder each round.
*   **The crank:** turning the crank with all four pegs loaded tests the whole set. A **correct** set opens the door. A **wrong** set grinds and shrieks (no information about which gear was wrong) and the phantom **strikes immediately**.

## Solution Matrix — DM ONLY (ANSWER KEY)

| Peg Position | Required Tooth Count | Constellation (stars on chart) | Riddle Line Reference |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Peg 1** | **4 Teeth** | The Bat (4 stars, under the arrow) | *"Where the arrow wakes the midnight sky, / The first wing's lights on Peg One lie."* |
| **Peg 2** | **7 Teeth** | The Dragon (7 stars) | *"The great wyrm coils through Two and Three —"* |
| **Peg 3** | **7 Teeth** | The Dragon again (the twin) | *"One beast, two pegs: alike they'll be."* |
| **Peg 4** | **3 Teeth** | The Scythe (3 stars) | *"The reaper's blade cuts last of all: / On Peg Four its lights bid the Phantom fall."* |
| **Check** | **4 + 7 + 7 + 3 = 21** | **XXI** in the chart's center | *"Let every star bite into brass; / The sky's heart counts the whole — then pass."* |

**Decoys:** the Raven (6 stars, another winged constellation; it tempts Peg 1), the Coffin (5 stars; it tempts the "reaper" line), and the Wolf (8 stars). A single decoy substitution breaks the 21 total (for example Raven: 6 + 7 + 7 + 3 = 23). Multiple substitutions can still total 21 (3 + 4 + 6 + 8), so the sum is only a cross-check; the riddle and order determine the solution.

## Hazard & Clockwork Phantom Mechanics — DM ONLY
*   **Clockwork Phantom:** AC 15, 22 HP, Wisdom save +0, flies. It can't be destroyed: at 0 HP it bursts into gears, skips its next strike, and reassembles.
*   **When it strikes:** on the real-time tick (first at 3:00, then every 2 minutes) and **immediately after any wrong crank**.
    *   *Scythe Sweep:* +5 to hit, reach 10 ft., **5 (1d6 + 2) slashing** damage. (Drac AC 14 / 38 HP; Johnny AC 13 / 38 HP. A typical run lands 2–4 hits, about 10–20 damage spread across both PCs.)
    *   If a target is below 10 HP, the phantom instead knocks one placed gear back into the pile.
*   **Interrupting the phantom** (full details in DM Notes → Adjudicating the character hooks):
    *   **Drac, Look Into My Eyes!:** Action, DC 15 Wis save. On a failure the phantom skips its next strike.
    *   **Johnny, DJ Backpack:** Reaction when the phantom attacks him. The phantom skips its next strike.
    *   **Johnny, Sick Beats:** once per station, cancels the strike from one wrong crank and resets the gears with no penalty.

## The 4 Progressive Clues — DM ONLY (read aloud; do not hand over)

### Clue 1: The Painted Dome (Sensory / Environmental)
*   **Trigger:** Minute 2–3, if they haven't connected the star chart to the riddle (or haven't found the chart yet).
*   **Read aloud:**
    > *The clock ticks and, high above, the painted dome flickers. For a heartbeat the constellations on the ceiling glow, exactly matching the brass star chart on the desk, and a thin golden line runs from the chart's arrow down to the little arrow above Peg 1.*
*   **Intended effect:** "The sky, the chart and the door are connected." It points them at the chart and at Peg 1's arrow, but gives no names or numbers.

### Clue 2: The Gilded Stars (Character Sheet Feature — Drac's Bat Form)
*   **Trigger:** Minute 5–6, when Drac uses **Bat Form** (bonus action) to fly up to the dome. Backup route: Johnny's **History DC 12**.
*   **Read aloud (Bat Form):**
    > *Up close, you see that only three of the six painted constellations are inlaid with real celestial brass: the little **Bat** right under the arrow, the long coiled **Dragon**, and the curved **Scythe**. The Raven, the Coffin and the Wolf are just dull grey paint.*
*   **Read aloud (Johnny, History):**
    > *You remember a song lyric, or maybe it was a video game: old Barovian stargazers called the Bat "the first wing" because it rises at midnight, the Dragon "the great wyrm," and the Scythe "the reaper's blade."*
*   **Intended effect:** settles the Raven-vs-Bat and Coffin-vs-Scythe arguments. It does not say how many stars each one has or what the stars are for.

### Clue 3: The Crank Inscription (Logic / Formula Constraint)
*   **Trigger:** Minute 8–9, when someone grabs or inspects the crank (or right after a first wrong crank).
*   **Read aloud:**
    > *Worn letters are stamped around the crank's hub: **STAR FOR TOOTH · TWO AND THREE TURN AS ONE · ALL TOGETHER: THE SKY'S HEART.***
*   **Optional add-on (Johnny's ear):** after any wrong crank, Johnny may make a **Performance check (DC 13)** to hear *how many* gears rang true in the grinding, without hearing *which* ones.
*   **Intended effect:** states the stars-to-teeth rule, the twin-gear rule and the sum check (21, from the chart's center) without giving any individual number.

### Clue 4: The Hypnotized Clockface (Emergency Fail-Forward)
*   **Trigger:** Only after a critical mistake (two wrong cranks in a row, or a PC below 10 HP) **or** at 11:00 if they're stuck. It's best delivered through Drac's **Look Into My Eyes!**; if Drac has no uses left, the phantom's own clock simply jams.
*   **Read aloud:**
    > *The phantom's clockface spins wildly and its ticking slows into words, the original clockmaker's rhyme: "Four in one... and three in four..." Then it freezes.*
*   **Intended effect:** gives Pegs 1 and 4 outright (the book's line). The twins rule plus the 21 total gives 7 + 7 in the middle. If they are **still** stuck at 13:00, the frozen phantom slowly points at each correct gear in turn.

## Visual Props & Artifacts Specification (Ready for Generation)
*Use these prompt descriptions for an image-generation agent or graphic designer to make the physical table assets. Items marked **PLAYER HANDOUT** go on the table; nothing else does.*

### 1. The Four-Peg Brass Vault Door Board (PLAYER HANDOUT / play mat)
*   **Dimensions/Format:** 11" x 8.5" horizontal play mat or printout.
*   **Art Style:** An intricate steampunk-gothic brass vault door with concentric planetary rings and four distinct circular mounting pegs labeled **"PEG 1"**, **"PEG 2"**, **"PEG 3"**, and **"PEG 4"** left to right. Space the peg circles about 2.6" apart (centers) and make each about 2.5" across, so four gears sit side by side across the 11" width without overlapping. A brass crank handle sits in the center. A small engraved arrow (↑) sits directly above **PEG 1**. No numbers or constellations anywhere else on the door.

### 2. The Seven Cogwheel Cutout Tokens (PLAYER HANDOUT)
*   **Dimensions/Format:** Circular cardboard or acrylic gear cutouts, 2" to 2.5" diameter (small enough that four fit side by side on the door board).
*   **Art Style:** Identical polished Barovian brass on every gear, with **no stamps, symbols or color differences on the front**. Large, bold triangular teeth with clear gaps, so they can be counted at a glance.
*   **Back of each token (DM only):** a small pencil letter and tooth count.
    *   **Gear A:** 4 teeth (Peg 1)
    *   **Gear B:** 7 teeth (Peg 2)
    *   **Gear C:** 7 teeth (Peg 3)
    *   **Gear D:** 3 teeth (Peg 4)
    *   **Decoy E:** 5 teeth · **Decoy F:** 6 teeth · **Decoy G:** 8 teeth
*   **Legibility note:** the 7- and 8-tooth gears look similar at speed. Make the teeth chunky, and make sure the 8 isn't simply the biggest gear. Mix the sizes so size doesn't track tooth count.

### 3. Handout Props & Standees
*   **Prop Handout 1 — The Gilded Door Plaque (PLAYER HANDOUT):** a heavy brass plaque card (7" x 4") with engraved, black-enameled gothic script, printed at 14pt or larger:
    > *Where the arrow wakes the midnight sky,*  
    > *The first wing's lights on Peg One lie.*  
    > *The great wyrm coils through Two and Three —*  
    > *One beast, two pegs: alike they'll be.*  
    > *The reaper's blade cuts last of all:*  
    > *On Peg Four its lights bid the Phantom fall.*  
    > *Let every star bite into brass;*  
    > *The sky's heart counts the whole — then pass.*
*   **Prop Handout 2 — The Astrolabe Star Chart (PLAYER HANDOUT):** a circular star chart, **10" diameter** (two players lean over it together), deep midnight blue with gold stars. A golden arrow (**↑**) sits on the rim at the 12 o'clock position, and the gold Roman numeral **XXI** sits inside a small sun emblem at the exact center. Six constellations are spaced like clock positions, each drawn as **1/4" gold star dots joined by thin lines, at least 1/2" apart and at least 3/4" in from the rim**, with a small gothic-caps label under each:
    *   **12 o'clock — THE BAT** (4 stars), directly beneath the arrow, wings spread.
    *   **2 o'clock — THE COFFIN** (5 stars).
    *   **4 o'clock — THE DRAGON** (7 stars) in a coiled S-shape. Keep the tail-tip star well clear of the rim and other lines; it's the one players miscount.
    *   **6 o'clock — THE RAVEN** (6 stars), wings spread (the intended Bat decoy).
    *   **8 o'clock — THE WOLF** (8 stars).
    *   **10 o'clock — THE SCYTHE** (3 stars) in a curved blade shape.
*   **Prop Handout 3 — Clockwork Phantom Standee (PLAYER HANDOUT):** a 5-inch vertical paper mini/standee showing an 8-foot-tall suit of floating gothic plate armor held together by whirring bronze gears, with a clockface in its chest, wielding an hourglass pendulum scythe.
*   **Prop Handout 4 — Observatory Crest #3 (PLAYER HANDOUT, award on solve):** a 3-inch circular metallic silver token showing a celestial sunburst with interlocking gear teeth along its edge.

## DM Tips & Running Recommendations
1.  **Keep phantom damage low:** 1d6+2 per hit is enough to create panic without threatening anyone. Use the "knock a gear loose" option instead of damage once a PC is under 10 HP.
2.  **Mark the gear backs:** write the letter and tooth count faintly on the back of each gear so you can check their answer in two seconds without counting.
3.  **Watch for the miscount:** the Dragon's seventh star is the one teens miss. If they put a 6 and a 7 on Pegs 2 and 3, don't correct them. Ask, "Does that match what the riddle says about Two and Three?"
4.  **Optional add-on (*Noon Mark*, Ch 12):** if the pair cracks the door before about 8:00, a stone obelisk rises from the floor ringed by **24 braziers numbered 1–24**. Read the obelisk's riddle from the printed book (its answer is **NOON**). The solution is to light **only brazier 1**, so the obelisk's shadow falls on **brazier 12**; that reveals a bonus healing potion for each PC. Don't say the answer or the brazier numbers aloud; just hand them the riddle. Cap it at 3 minutes.
