## 4. Physical Prop Usability Report
*   **Did the visual cues guide thinking?** Yes. Once Clue 1 landed, script-matching by eye was faster than rolling: Owen matched two cards with no roll. The physical act of laying a card next to its tag made the "five of eight" idea obvious. Reading the English under every rack (Jayden's "smnagrhe") was a useful wrong first step, because it showed the order mattered.
*   **Elvish vs Sylvan decoy:** worked as intended (cost about 1.5 minutes) but only because Owen could see the open vs closed loops at arm's length. If printed small or blurry it becomes unfair. **Make the loop difference bold.**
*   **Ceiling tracker:** the 16-notch, 1-ft track was easy to read. The token got nudged once when Jayden leaned over the mat. **Use a paperclip slider.**
*   **Sand timer:** the 1-minute lockout timer on the table was a good visible cue; Owen checked it on his own before letting Jayden speak the password.
*   **Standees and tokens:** tipping Wayne's standee over for prone was a free laugh. The Frank standee under the boss made "Frank can't move" clear without rules talk. The crest token was a satisfying hand-off.
*   **Card count:** 8 tags + 5 word cards + 8 penalty cards is the right amount; the penalty cards stayed in the DM's hand until rolled, so the table wasn't cluttered.
*   **Legibility risk:** if the tags are placed on the mat before players arrive, keep the English text small so they don't read SNARE straight off the board in corridor order *before* noticing that only five count (the decoy letters M, G, H break it up, which is why this works).

## 5. DM Tuning Recommendations
### Pacing for live play
*   **The slips are the padding.** Without the three slips (about 2 minutes) and Jayden's nat 20, this pair would have finished around 9–10 minutes. A very careful pair that never slips could come in under 10. Bait gently (see coaching notes) to make sure at least one or two slips happen.
*   **Variance from Investigation rolls:** Frank's Int −1 and Wayne's Int +0 mean a cold streak could add 2–3 minutes. If two Investigation rolls fail in a row, remind them they can match the scripts by eye (*"Do any of those squiggles look like anything else in the room?"*).
*   **Hold Clue 3 until 8:00** even if the pair has all five matches early. Let them try to work out the order themselves first.
*   **Frank's sheet line:** expect Frank's player to read *Massive Shoulders* aloud. It reliably causes a slip and a big laugh. Don't warn them.

### Emergency time-savers if the group runs slow
1.  **At 06:00 and no brace yet:** say *"Frank, look up. All those pistons meet at one point."* (Clue 2 without a roll.)
2.  **At 09:00 with fewer than 4 matches:** let any match be automatic by eye, no Investigation roll.
3.  **At 11:00 still no password:** Clue 4 Variant A (the words fall onto their racks).
4.  **At 12:00 crest not retrieved:** Clue 4 Variant B, and the pups go straight down the right pipe.
5.  **At 13:00:** skip Frank's exit save and narrate the dive.
6.  **At 15:00:** the ceiling hits 0 ft; use the fail-forward crush (everyone tumbles out with the crest, 2d6 damage).

## Changes Applied
The simulation turned up these issues, and they have been fixed in the station file:
1.  **Swarm of Pups duration was undefined.** A wrong pipe would have used up Wayne's only use and dead-ended the station. **Fix:** one use keeps the pups around for the whole station; a wrong pipe costs about a minute (Solution step 4 and the hook notes).
2.  **Frank's player idle while braced.** **Fix:** coaching notes now say Frank can't move or act, but his player can still talk and handle the cards.
3.  **Elvish/Sylvan decoy legibility.** **Fix:** prop spec 6 now requires closed (Elvish) vs open (Sylvan) loops readable at arm's length.
4.  **Ceiling token got bumped.** **Fix:** prop spec 1 now calls for a paperclip or clip-on slider.
5.  **Wrong password had no ruling** (Jayden's "Open sesame"). **Fix:** Room Setup now says wrong passwords only make the stone mouth cough dust.
